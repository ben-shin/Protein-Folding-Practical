Model assumptions & interpretation

The two-state model uses linear folded and unfolded baselines with ΔGunfold([D]) = ΔG°H₂O − m[D] and Cm = ΔG°H₂O/m. Thermodynamic interpretation requires independent evidence of equilibration and reversible two-state behaviour.

The logistic model describes a transition with constant baselines. Its mathematical relationship to the two-state curve does not establish a physical mechanism.

Standard errors come from local covariance and are approximate. Poor concentration coverage or identifiability cannot be repaired by a small fitting error. Multiple initial guesses, baseline coverage, parameter bounds and covariance diagnostics help identify limitations.

AIC, AICc and BIC assume independent Gaussian errors with one common variance estimated from the data. Parameter counts include this variance. Comparisons use identical retained observations and the same response. Undefined AICc is shown as unavailable.
